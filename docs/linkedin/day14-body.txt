Speed of sound in a 0.65-gravity gas at 2,000 psi and 150 °F:

441.0 m/s if you assume ideal gas
410.0 m/s if you carry Z

7.5 %, and it goes straight into your Mach number.

The reason is one line. c = √(kZRT/M), so c scales as √Z. Setting Z = 1 when it is really 0.8646 inflates the answer by 1/√0.8646 = 1.0755. The error IS the square root of the compressibility you left out.

But I swept the pressure range through the card before writing this, and the interesting part is not the 7.5 %. It is the shape.

200 psi — 1.2 %
1,000 psi — 5.1 %
2,250 psi — 7.7 % ← worst
4,000 psi — 3.1 %

The error does not grow with pressure. It peaks and comes back down, because it peaks exactly where Z bottoms out — here at 2,250 psi, Z = 0.8626. Not a coincidence: the gap simply IS 1/√Z − 1.

So "ideal gas gets worse the higher you go" is half right, and the useful half is narrower: it is worst in the Z-minimum band. For a 0.65 gas at 150 °F that band is roughly 1,500–3,000 psi — ordinary transmission and gas-processing pressures, not exotic ones.

One thing the plot shows better than a number: the ideal-gas line is flat. At fixed temperature c = √(kRT/M) has no pressure dependence — it moved 0.016 m/s across the sweep. All the curvature is Z.

The honest boundary, because this is the kind of number people lift into a report: c = √(kZRT/M) is a first-order real-gas correction. It carries Z but not Z's pressure and temperature derivatives, which a rigorous sonic velocity needs. Enough to prove that ignoring Z here costs several per cent; not a substitute for an equation of state. The card says SCREENING for that reason.

Where does this bite you? Flare headers, blowdown lines, relief tailpipes — the places you are already sitting close to Mach 0.7 and the margin you think you have is the margin you do not.
