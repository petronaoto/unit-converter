The most valuable thing an engineering tool can do is stop.

NPSHa gives no pass/fail
Available head and the three terms behind it — and nothing else. NPSHr lives on the vendor's tested curve; the margin on top is HI 9.6.1 and API 610, both licensed.

2
The steam card refuses IF97 Region 3
Dense near-critical water — 350–590 °C above 16.5 MPa — needs a different formulation with density iteration. Regions 1, 2 and 4 only.
not covered — not extrapolated
3
Over-range orifices are marked, not clamped
Exceed API 526 T (26.00 in²) and the card returns the actual required area beside a flagged letter. The worst behaviour available is rounding an impossible requirement down.
T+  ·  area as calculated
4
Correlations warn at their edges
Papay is valid for 0 < Pr ≤ 15 and 1.05 ≤ Tr ≤ 3.0. Outside it the result is flagged extrapolated, not quietly returned. Same for Lee-Gonzalez-Eakin viscosity.
⚠ outside validity — extrapolated
5
Missing coefficients stay missing
The Crane TP-410 set omits gradual reducers — their coefficients could not be verified against a primary source. And a liquid service on a two-phase result gets no verdict at all.
PHASE MISMATCH
6
Stop using this; open the simulator
Everything here is screening. When a number checks a design that is right. When it sets the design — especially when two-phase behaviour is the answer, not a correction to it — you want an equation of state, a flowsheet and someone's PTC 10 data.
a tool for the first hour, not the last
A tool that names its own ceiling beats one pretending it has none.
