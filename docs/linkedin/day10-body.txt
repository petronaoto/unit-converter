Three weeks after handover, someone asks which roughness you used.

That question is the whole problem with engineering numbers, and most tools answer it with a shrug: the result is in the document, the input is in somebody's memory.

So the export puts them on the same page. Section 2 of the report this tool produces carries pipe ID, length, elevation, roughness, erosion C-factor, two-phase method and both phase flows — beside total ΔP, velocity, Reynolds, Darcy f and the erosional check. One run, one page.

Which is only worth anything if the two still match.

That is the part I would actually steal. Touch any input on the ΔP card and the badge becomes:

Two-Phase (HEM) · inputs changed — recalculate

in amber — and the figures stay on screen. It does not blank the card. Blanking would be tidier and worse: you would lose the number you were reading and learn nothing from it. Labelling it means the screen can never quietly show a result beside inputs that no longer produced it.

Now the part a post about traceability has to include, or it is the same failure in a smaller font.

The report covers seven sections, but Basic Eng carries five of its nine cards. Steam, NPSHa, Compressor and the Gas Property Estimator do not export. Neither does the fittings / line-sizing row of the ΔP card — so last Friday's 695.85 Pa of fitting losses is on screen and not in the document.

That is a real gap. It is written on the graphic because you should know it before you rely on it, not after.

How does your team record which inputs produced a number — a controlled sheet, a PDF in the job file, or somebody's memory? And has a stale result ever made it into something you signed?
