WHY THE MASS BASIS BARELY MOVES
Methane has the highest heating value per kilogram of any alkane, so the leanest LNG (Trinidad Pt Fortin, HHV 41.05) carries the most energy per tonne — and is also the least dense (431.02 kg/m³). Richer LNG is denser and hotter per unit volume at once, so on a mass basis the two effects very nearly cancel. It is top of the per-tonne column and bottom of the per-m³ column.
The factor treated as the sloppiest is the stable one. Mass basis moves 1.6 % across these five cargoes; the two volume bases move 6.7 % and 10.4 %.
So the trap was never the factor — it is the basis change nobody announces: tonnes in the shipping email, m³ on the terminal sheet, MMBtu on the invoice, one number carried between them. 1 Nm³ = 37.3258 scf stays definitional and never drifts; everything from mass or volume to energy is not a conversion at all but a composition, frozen at one moment. And 1.6 % is small only until you scale it: applied to a nominal 80,000-tonne cargo, the gap between the lowest and highest of these five factors is 66,000 MMBtu. (80,000 t is a stated round basis — each origin loads a different mass into the same hull.)
